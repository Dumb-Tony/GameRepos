# 11 · Worldbuilding & Lore

## The island: Vessakai

A stratovolcano island (~30 km) in the far Pacific, absent from every chart for one
reason the player slowly triangulates: **the Hum**.

## The Hum (the load-bearing mystery)

A vast seam of **heartglass** — a piezoelectric crystalline mineral unique to Vessakai —
threads Mother Ash and the island's roots. Twice daily, tidewater surges through the
Gullet's sea-caves and *squeezes* the seam; the strain discharges as a powerful,
slow-pulsing electromagnetic field: a seven-beat infrasonic chorus.

Consequences, all of which the player experiences before understanding:
- Compasses spin and radios drown within ~50 miles (why ships and planes end up here,
  and why nothing that arrives gets to report back).
- Surveys and satellites log the region as instrument-error noise (why no chart admits it).
- The lagoon's bioluminescent plankton bloom on the pulse (why the water glows).
- Wildlife navigates by it — the almanac's "impossible species" arrived down the Hum's
  corridor like a lighthouse only animals can read; the Ch6 exodus is the Hum faltering.
- Fifty years ago, people came to study it. That went badly.

The Hum is fully explicable *mechanically* — Vane's journals get the player 90% there —
but the last 10% (the Tidewell, the door below, what "answers" at king tide, X5's other
island) stays deliberately, permanently ajar. The mystery resolves emotionally, never
completely.

## The Kaari (living, hidden)

Perhaps three hundred people in the Inner Green: terrace farmers, cave-keepers,
tide-priests, watchers. Not primitives and not mystics-in-the-fog — a small, wary,
technically ingenious culture (tidal clocks, heartglass lamps, rope-bridge engineering)
that made one civilizational choice long ago: *smallness over discovery.* Their law is
the covenant; their fear is Halcyon happening again; their internal argument (dramatized
in the Ch6 council) is Naia's generation asking whether hiding is still tending.
Glyph stones scattered island-wide are their old public record — history, recipes,
jokes, grief — and the player's Rosetta path into all of it.

## Ecosystems & almanac (58 species — selection)

- **Reef & lagoon:** day octopus (Nine's kin) · reef shark · parrotfish · giant clam ·
  turtle rookery · the glowing plankton · moray · pearl oysters
- **Shore & mangrove:** saltwater crocodile (Old Grin) · mudskippers · fiddler-crab
  nations (J2's electorate) · herons · sea eagles (Vela) · frigatebirds
- **Jungle:** crab-eating macaques (Ipo's troop) · red junglefowl (Moa's kind) ·
  bearded pigs (Buri, the Boar King) · island dogs (Kavi's pack) · monitor lizard ·
  fruit bats · hornbills · tree snakes · stick insects the size of forearms
- **Highlands & caldera:** swiftlets (edible nests, cliff content) · feral goats
  (Halcyon's escapees — invasive-species side quest with real Regard consequences) ·
  the white boar (omen, almanac entry #58, rarely *confirmed*)
- **"Impossible" entries (7):** species with no business here — a lone albatross that
  never leaves, freshwater eels of a species from 4,000 miles away… each one is Hum
  lore wearing feathers or scales.

**Flora:** breadfruit, taro, wild banana, sugarcane relics of Kaari agriculture gone
feral (edible archaeology — the Terrace *is* a seed bank); feverbark grove (Edda's
transplant, the malaria-analog cure); strangler figs (Canopy Roads' living
architecture); the tide-orchids that only open on the Hum's pulse.

**Ecosystem simulation (lightweight but real):** hunting/fishing pressure depletes
regional populations visibly; goat spread degrades highland forage if unmanaged; Old
Grin's death triggers the rat tide; turtle-nest protection pays off in a Ch7 beach
full of hatchlings. Island Regard is computed from exactly these behaviors — the
"the island is watching" theme is mechanically true.

## Weather patterns

Trade-wind **Dry season** (Ch1–4): hot clear days, evening squalls, drought risk
inland. **Monsoon** (Ch5–6): the Long Rain, cyclone risk, flooded lowlands, the Gullet's
surge-lull rhythm. Microclimates: the caldera brews its own cloud; the north shore
takes the ocean's anger raw (hence Bone Beach); the Inner Green sits in engineered
calm — the Kaari chose their refuge with nine centuries of weather sense.

## Hidden lore threads (for writers)

1. **The seven-beat chorus** is not constant; it has *changed* three times in recorded
   glyph history. Each change preceded an arrival. It changed again the night the
   player's plane went down. (Never stated flat; assembled from glyphs 22, 27, 30 +
   Vane page 19 + Naia's one slip.)
2. **Edda's grave rows:** Vane's headstone faces the sea; the rooster's faces the grove.
   Edda jokes about it exactly once, and the joke is the saddest line in the game.
3. **The stranger's photograph** (prologue salvage / Nine's gift): the man in it was a
   courier, the case was his, and fragment 9 shows he'd photographed the island — from
   the *deck of a ship* — decades before the player's crash. The Hum lets some people go.
   It has reasons. (Feeds P1's "right of return" theme and X5.)
4. **What the Tidewell holds** is written only in negative space. The design bible's
   official answer is: *the island's memory, under pressure, since before the Kaari.*
   No scene may state more.
